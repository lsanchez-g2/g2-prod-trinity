# GetApp

This file is the operating context for product decisions in GetApp. Load when the task is anchored in GetApp. Do not cross-reference other G2 group brands unless the user explicitly asks.

## What GetApp is

GetApp is a software discovery platform with a comparison-first, research-deep posture. Founded 2010 in **Barcelona** by Christophe Primault and Jaime Sicam, acquired by Gartner 2015, now part of G2 Digital Markets after the G2 acquisition closed February 2026.

- **Audience**: tech-forward SMB and mid-market buyers, comparison-driven, research-mature
- **Region skew**: EU-leaning (legacy of Barcelona origins)
- **Lead model**: PPC (similar to Capterra but with a more research-focused buyer mix)
- **Award system**: GetApp Category Leaders (annual, by category)
- **Scale**: ~1.4M visits/month; ~50,000+ products across 900+ categories

## What buyers come for

The typical GetApp buyer is more research-mature than the Capterra average:

1. Often arrives with a shortlist already in mind, or comparing 3-5 specific products
2. Goes deep into comparison tools, feature mapping, pricing pages
3. Reads reviews more carefully, including longer ones
4. Uses GetApp's comparison features as a primary surface, not just product profile pages
5. May cross-check on G2 or directly with vendors before deciding

GetApp buyers tend to be:
- Already comfortable with SaaS, knowledgeable about category
- Comparing products explicitly, not just discovering them
- More likely to be technical (IT, ops, product, engineering)
- More EU-distributed than other G2 group brands
- More likely to value depth (long comparison tables, feature granularity) over breadth

## What vendors come for

Vendors invest in GetApp for visibility in a more research-oriented buyer pool. The mechanics:

- **Paid placement (PPC)**: bidding on category pages, similar to Capterra; CPCs typically lower due to lower competition
- **Profile depth**: GetApp values feature mapping more visibly; complete profiles convert better
- **Comparison surface visibility**: appearing in side-by-side comparison results matters more here than on Capterra
- **Category Leaders awards**: annual, used in vendor marketing
- **Cross-platform sync**: a review submitted on Capterra also appears on GetApp; vendor profile data is shared

### Vendor-facing

- Lead and traffic analytics

## Non-negotiable constraints

- **Review integrity**: same as the rest of the group
- **SEO and AEO surface health**: organic traffic is the primary acquisition channel; AI-driven search increasingly important
- **Comparison surface integrity**: this is GetApp's differentiating UX; if comparison tools become noisy, biased, or shallow, the brand loses its identity
- **Vendor commercial fairness**: PPC auction transparency
- **EU regulatory surface**: GDPR, EU accessibility regulations, EU AI Act considerations weigh more here than on US-skewed brands
- **Category and feature taxonomy**: feature-level granularity is part of the value proposition; degrading it weakens the comparison surface

## Common pushbacks specific to GetApp

A GetApp-savvy trio will push back when:

- A buyer surface change weakens the comparison tool or its prominence
- An AI feature is proposed without naming its EU regulatory posture (especially for AI Act risk classification, transparency, automated decision-making)
- The proposal assumes US-style buyer behavior (self-serve, fast, low-research) when GetApp buyers go deeper
- Localization is treated as translation rather than as a product surface in itself
- Feature-level granularity is being collapsed to simplify a screen, eroding the differentiator
- A change to comparison ranking does not have a vendor communication plan
- The proposal ignores that GetApp's buyer mix is more technical and less impressed by surface-level marketing copy
- Mobile is dismissed as secondary when EU mobile usage patterns differ from US
- The proposal references G2 group consolidation as if it were live; GetApp remains an independent product
