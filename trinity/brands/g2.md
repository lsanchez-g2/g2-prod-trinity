# G2

This file is the operating context for product decisions in G2. Load when the task is anchored in G2 (the original G2 marketplace, not the parent company). Do not cross-reference G2 Digital Markets brands (Capterra, Software Advice, GetApp) unless the user explicitly asks.

## What G2 is

G2 is the largest peer-to-peer software review marketplace, focused on mid-market and enterprise software buyers. Founded 2012, now the parent company of the G2 group after acquiring Capterra, Software Advice, and GetApp from Gartner in February 2026.

- **Audience**: mid-market and enterprise software buyers; technical and business buyers; buying committees
- **Region skew**: global, US-strong but with broad enterprise reach
- **Lead model**: subscription tiers for vendors (free profile up to enterprise contracts) + intent data sales + ads
- **Award system**: G2 Grid (Leader, High Performer, Contender, Niche) and various reports (Crowd, Best of, etc.)
- **Scale**: largest in the group by review depth and enterprise buyer engagement; G2.ai is the AI-driven recommendation engine

## What buyers come for

The G2 buyer is typically deeper into the buying process than a Capterra buyer:

1. Often arrives with a category shortlist or a specific product in mind
2. Reads long-form, peer-written reviews with detailed experience descriptions
3. Cross-checks the Grid position to validate the category narrative
4. Reads competitive comparisons (G2 supports detailed competitor analysis)
5. May download Crowd reports or Best of category reports
6. Is part of a buying committee, so material gets shared internally
7. Increasingly uses G2.ai for AI-driven recommendations and synthesis

G2 buyers tend to be:
- Mid-market or enterprise (larger orgs, more stakeholders)
- More technical and analytical
- Looking for peer validation from similar-sized companies and similar industries
- More likely to convert on a longer cycle, with multiple touches
- Sensitive to peer review depth and recency, less to badges alone

## What vendors come for

G2's vendor relationship is fundamentally different from Capterra/SA/GetApp:

- **Subscription model**: tiered profiles (Free, Standard, Pro, Power) with feature gating
- **Intent data**: G2 sells in-market buyer signal data to vendors for outbound and ABM
- **Ads**: targeted advertising on category pages and competitor pages
- **Grid placement**: drives a meaningful share of vendor narrative; Grid quadrant is used in marketing
- **Reports and content**: vendors invest in being included in Best of, Crowd reports, and category reports
- **Review collection**: vendors actively run review collection campaigns (often with G2's tools)
- **Competitive intelligence**: G2 surfaces competitive comparisons that vendors monitor closely

## Non-negotiable constraints

- **Review integrity**: peer-driven trust is the asset; tighter verification than typical (LinkedIn, work email, screenshots, video, even AI-detection)
- **Grid methodology integrity**: the Grid is a brand asset; methodology changes affect every vendor and must be defensible
- **SEO and AEO surface health**: organic search drives traffic; AI-driven search and answer-engine visibility increasingly load-bearing (G2.ai is partly an AEO play)
- **Vendor subscription value**: each tier must deliver enough value to justify its price; tier confusion or value erosion creates churn
- **Intent data quality and ethics**: intent data sold to vendors must be accurate and used in ways consistent with buyer expectations
- **Buying committee surfaces**: G2 must support the multi-stakeholder buyer journey (shareable links, exportable comparisons, downloadable reports)
- **Enterprise buyer trust**: this audience is harder to acquire and quicker to leave; perceived bias or low quality damages the asset

## Common pushbacks specific to G2

A G2-savvy trio will push back when:

- The buyer surface treats users like SMB self-serve buyers when G2 audience skews mid-market and enterprise
- A change ignores the buying committee dynamic (shareability, multi-user views, exportability)
- Grid methodology is being changed without modeling the vendor-narrative impact across categories
- Intent data is being expanded without addressing buyer-side transparency and trust
- AI features are being shipped without an eval set scaled to G2's review corpus and category depth
- A vendor-side change does not consider the subscription tier interaction effects
- The metric being optimized is engagement when the underlying need is decision quality
- A buyer-surface change degrades long-form review reading or category Grid prominence
- The proposal references G2 group consolidation as if it were live in product surfaces; G2 remains its own product
